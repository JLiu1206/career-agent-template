## What they do
- Global engineering consultancy founded in 1946 by Ove Arup as Ove N Arup Consulting Engineers [1]. It describes itself as "an independent, employee-led organisation" [2]. Its history page says ownership passed from the partners to a trust run by and for employees in 1977 [1].
- In the year to March 2026, turnover rose 5% to £2.3bn. The UK was its largest market at £674m, and it had more than 17,200 staff, excluding 1,100 Hong Kong staff [3].
- Its 2025–2030 strategy prioritises water infrastructure, the energy transition, data centres and transport [4].
- Its bridge work covers road, rail and pedestrian bridges, inspection and testing, life extension and repurposing, digital twins and structural health monitoring. Named UK examples include the Queensferry Crossing (joint venture) and the Sharnbrook Viaduct strengthening [5][6].

- **5 Jul 2026 (last updated):** an Arup and Mott MacDonald joint venture began design and construction consultancy for Hong Kong's Tsing Lung Bridge. It is a dual four-lane sea crossing with a main span of about 1.3 km [13].

## Specific hooks
- **Fatigue in ageing steel bridges.** Arup led guidance for National Highways, with The Welding Institute (TWI), on evaluating fatigue risk in steel bridges. The work is feeding a new industry standard. More than half of National Highways' bridges were designed before BS 5400 Part 10 (1980) [15]. An Arup article from October 2025 calls metal fatigue in bridge decks "a key design dimension" [16]. On the M4 Boston Manor Viaduct, Arup's emergency repair of cracked electroslag welds in 2012 let it reopen six days after closure. A later bearing replacement extended the life of the welds and bearings by 60 years [17]. A graduate with coursework or a project in fatigue, fracture or steel design could link it to this work.
- **Data-led assessment of existing bridges.** On Sharnbrook Viaduct, a 176m Victorian rail bridge on the Midland Main Line, Arup built a workflow for Network Rail and BAM covering inspection, defect review, repair and carbon reporting. It used Arup's Bridge Taxonomy linked to a 3D BIM model, a Fuse dashboard and One Click LCA [18]. This could link to inspection, surveying, BIM or scripting experience.
- **Low-carbon footbridge design.** Arup and Seán Harrington Architects won the Lower Thames Crossing low-carbon footbridge competition. The design removes concrete from the bridge and its approach ramps and adds planting for biodiversity, at the A127 (page last updated 20 May 2025) [19]. This fits the "Changemakers" attribute [7].
- **Gull Wing Bridge, Lowestoft.** Arup was structural designer for Suffolk County Council. The 350m crossing of Lake Lothing has a 38.5m rolling bascule span, described as "the largest in the world to be raised by hydraulic cylinders". IStructE judges noted how it integrates structural, mechanical, hydraulic, electrical and control systems [20]. It features in The Arup Journal 2026 Issue 1 (May 2026) [21], and it illustrates "total architecture" [2].
- **80th year.** Arup was founded in 1946 [1], so 2026 is its 80th year. In November 2025 the CEO said "We're approaching our 80th anniversary" [4].
- **Road network framework.** Arup is a Tier 1 supplier, with AECOM, on National Highways' SPaTS3 framework, worth up to £495m and expected to run to 2031 (3 July 2025) [22].

# Style profile: Ellis Varga (fictional demo student)

Built from four exemplars, 2025-03 to 2026-10. The 2026 documents are the
best guide to the current voice.

## 1. Voice summary

- Plain and specific. Ellis says what happened, with the number, then what it meant.
- Mostly short sentences, with one longer one per paragraph to carry the detail.
- Modest. There are no superlatives, and claims rest on results rather than adjectives.
- British spelling (organised, programme, modelling).
- Ends on a practical, forward-looking line, never a summary of what was just said.

## 2. Structure by type

- **application-answer:** 3 to 4 short paragraphs. It opens on the concrete
  situation, not on the question restated. Action and result come with numbers.
  The last paragraph says what Ellis took from it or wants next. There's no
  sign-off.
- **cover-letter:** "Dear Hiring Team," then a one-line purpose, a story
  paragraph, a paragraph on why this firm with one specific detail, a skills
  paragraph, a two-line close and "Kind regards,".
- **statement:** a short title, then an honest reason, why this project, what
  Ellis can offer (with an example), and what Ellis expects to do.

## 3. Diction and register

- Contractions ("I'd", "I've", "didn't") in answers and statements written
  since 2026. None in the 2025 cover letter.
- Numbers as digits with units: 18%, 40 bearings, 6 degree, 94%.
- Common verbs: ran, built, wrote, checked, talked through, tested.
- Hedges only where honest: "about three hours", "should move".

## 4. Signature moves

- A short fragment that sharpens the previous sentence: "A small span, a
  tight budget and a site that has to stay open." "Not the checks themselves,
  which were slow, but working out how to make them faster."
- Learning tied to seeing the real thing: "I learn faster when I can see the
  thing I am calculating."
- A small contrast in the close: "getting properly good at the work rather
  than starting again somewhere new."

## 5. Things Ellis does not do

- No em dashes.
- No openers like "I am passionate about" or "From a young age".
- No praise of the firm that would fit any firm. There's always one specific
  detail, or nothing.
- No bullet points or headings inside answers.
- Never claims sole credit for a team result: "our first design", "we moved
  to".

## Genre exemptions

The voice pass must keep these:

- The single tricolon in the cover letter ("A small span, a tight budget and a
  site that has to stay open") is Ellis's own. One such list per document is
  fine.
- Confident first-person results with figures ("my Python script ran ... in
  about three hours instead of two days").
- "Dear Hiring Team," and "Kind regards," in letters.
- The short sentence fragments described under signature moves.

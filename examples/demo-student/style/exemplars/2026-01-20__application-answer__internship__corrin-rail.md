Tell us about a time you solved a problem as part of a team. (250 words)

In my second-year design module our team of four had to build a small wind turbine and hit a target power output in the wind tunnel. Two days before testing, our blades kept stalling at low wind speeds and we were producing about half the target.

The team wanted to remake the blades with a different profile. I didn't think we had time to print and test a new set, so I suggested we check the cheaper fix first. I changed the pitch angle in our spreadsheet model and found that a 6 degree change should move the stall point well below the test speed. I talked the others through the numbers and we agreed to try it before reprinting anything.

We adjusted the hub that evening. In the wind tunnel the next morning we reached 94% of the target output, and we used the spare day to tidy our report rather than rush a reprint.

What I took from it is to test the smallest change that could work before committing to the big one, and to show the team the numbers rather than just my opinion. I've tried to keep that habit on every project since.
